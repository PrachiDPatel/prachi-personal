a note on honesty

one sphere this time, and one scale. every star sits at its true direction on the celestial sphere — J2000 catalog, real RA and Dec, north up. the old plates had to flatten the sky onto paper; this one doesn't.

star sizes are exaggerated so a finger can find them. spin it: the whole sky wheels, and the figures keep their shapes from every side. milky way photo: NASA/Goddard Space Flight Center Scientific Visualization Studio, from Gaia DR2 (ESA/Gaia/DPAC) — public domain.

polaris · the observer

i put myself at polaris — the observer. the whole sky wheels around it and it doesn't move. that's the job: stay put, watch everything.

field notes

mizar & alcor sit 12′ apart — the pair that watches. lens looks outward; heliotrope looks inward.

merope is the lost pleiad of myth, the sister who dims her light; here she keeps "project hail mary, twice".

sheliak dims every 12.9 days — two stars crossing, an eclipse you can set a watch by.

the pleiades sit where they truly sit. lean in close: nine facets of one person.

the band crossing the sphere is the real milky way — 1.7 billion stars from the gaia survey, recolored cool to this palette, sitting in its true place. m31 smudges in the dark to the north-east.

the figures spread across the whole turning sky now — winter, spring, summer, autumn. scorpius sat this one out anyway.
